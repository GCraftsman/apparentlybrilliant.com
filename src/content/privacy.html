Privacy Policy

Effective November 26, 2026

This policy describes how Apparently Brilliant, LLC (“Apparently Brilliant,” “we,” “us”) handles information when you use our iPhone and iPad apps and this website, apparentlybrilliant.com.

Our apps are built so that, by default, they do not collect personal data. If a particular app’s practices differ from that default, the app will say so in its App Store privacy label and, when appropriate, inside the app. Those disclosures describe that app, and this policy will be updated to match.

Who we are

Apparently Brilliant, LLC is a Texas single-member limited liability company. Its owner is Michael Lacasse. We publish apps on the Apple App Store.

Privacy questions: michael@apparentlybrilliant.com.

What our apps collect

Unless a specific app tells you otherwise:

  • We do not ask you to create an account.
  • We do not collect your name, email address, contacts, photos, precise location, or identifiers for advertising.
  • We do not sell personal information.
  • We do not use third-party advertising, analytics, or tracking tools.
  • We do not build profiles for advertising.

An app may store information only on your device, such as settings or content you create. That information stays on the device. We do not receive it. If you delete the app, the device removes that local information, subject to how your own backups work.

If a future app needs to collect or transmit personal data, we will describe that collection in the app and in its App Store privacy disclosures, and we will update this policy.

Apple, the App Store, and analytics

We distribute our apps through Apple. When you download, buy, or restore an app, Apple handles that transaction under its own privacy policy. We do not receive your Apple ID password, payment card number, or billing address.

Apple may show us aggregated information in App Store Connect, such as download counts, session counts, and the kinds of devices and operating system versions in use. We use those summaries to keep apps working. They are not used to identify you, and they are not used for advertising.

If you choose to share analytics or diagnostics with Apple, or with app developers, that choice is controlled in iOS Settings and by Apple. Crash reports Apple shares with us can include technical details about the device and what the app was doing when it failed. We use them to fix defects.

Apple’s privacy policy is at apple.com/legal/privacy.

This website

apparentlybrilliant.com is a static website. We do not run accounts on it. We do not set our own cookies, and we do not add analytics or advertising.

Pages are served by Cloudflare. When your browser requests a page, Cloudflare receives ordinary connection data, such as IP address, user agent, and the page requested, in order to deliver the site and protect it from abuse. We do not use that information for advertising, and we do not add tracking of our own.

When you email us

If you email michael@apparentlybrilliant.com, we receive the address you write from, your message, and any attachments you include. We use that information to reply and to provide support. We do not add you to a mailing list because you contacted us.

We keep correspondence for as long as we need it to handle your request and for ordinary business records, then we delete it. Please do not send passwords or full payment card numbers.

Children’s privacy

Our apps and this website are not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children. If you believe a child has sent us personal information, email michael@apparentlybrilliant.com and we will delete it.

If we ever publish an app directed to children, we will update this policy and that app’s App Store disclosures first.

Your choices

You can use our apps without creating an account. You can control Apple’s analytics and diagnostics sharing in Settings on your device. You can email us to ask what we hold from messages you sent, or to ask us to delete that correspondence.

Changes to this policy

We may update this policy when our practices change. We will post the new version at apparentlybrilliant.com/privacy and change the effective date above. If an app’s data practices change in a material way, we will also update that app’s App Store privacy disclosures.
